# Objection Handling

> **For collegeworkscoach.com knowledge base.** Save as `knowledge/objections.md` (new file). Combines the official 2026 universal handlers with top-performer field responses. Use when evaluating any moment in the transcript where the homeowner raises a concern, hesitation, or condition.

## Conditions vs. Objections (Critical Distinction)

95% of "conditions" are objections in disguise. Do not be fooled by these statements.

**Conditions (the disguise):**

- "I always get other estimates"
- "That's out of my budget"
- "I need to talk with my wife/husband"

**Actual objections (what they really mean):**

- "I'll think about it"
- "We want to get other estimates"
- "The price seems higher than we thought"
- "We are going to do it ourselves"
- "I don't think we really need it right now"
- "We are not ready to commit right now"
- "We think we want someone more professional"

The four real categories underneath every objection:

1. Need
2. Urgency
3. Trust
4. Price

An objection is an opportunity to sell. Treat it as an invitation to address the real concern, not a closed door.

## Universal Objection Handlers (Memorize These)

These four phrases work on almost every objection. They reframe rather than argue.

1. **"Are you sure? It is just a free estimate."**
2. **"What we normally do is..."** (hand out a flyer, provide your number, suggest a follow-up)
3. **"Well, did you notice..."** (point out an area that needs attention, even if they say they don't need the service)
4. **"Wouldn't you like to see how much it costs for a professional to do it?"** (compares pro service to DIY, neighbor, kid)

## "I'll Think About It" (NOT a Real Objection)

This is the most common stall. Run the 5-Step Close (see methodology.md).

If they say yes to the company, yes to the person, yes to the quality, yes to the price, then "do you want this job done this year?" becomes the only remaining question.

## "We Want to Get Other Estimates"

- "You can get a better price, but you can't get the same quality. I showed you the standards."
- "I can be the cheapest or the most expensive. I have custom options for you."
- Pivot to service: "Better quality is a better investment. Higher price, but it will last longer. Do you want to paint it every two years, or every 8-10?"

## "The Price Seems Higher Than We Thought"

- "Products like paint are the same, but service is not. Not all paint jobs are the same."
- "It's not like shopping for a TV or microwave where everything is the same."
- "If you want a lower price, I can give you one with a different option. But you can't get the lowest price AND the best quality."

## "We Are Going to Do It Ourselves"

"Wouldn't you like to see how much it costs for a professional to do it?"

Then compare the professional service to using yourself, a friend, or a kid. The real cost is time plus risk plus redo, not just materials.

## "We Don't Need Our Home Painted Right Now"

"Well, did you notice [specific issue, like fading or peeling on a side you've already pointed out]?"

If they need it: point out what needs to be done, reiterate it is completely FREE, ask again.

If they truly don't need it: "I totally get it. We also offer estimates for decks, fences, and interiors. Could I interest you in one of those?"

## "I Need to Talk to My Spouse First"

This is a setting problem, not an objection. Should have been caught at the I-Call.

In the moment: "Of course! What we normally do is..." (hand them a flyer, give them your number, suggest you call back when both decision-makers are home).

Prevention: at the I-Call, confirm both decision-makers will be present for the full 90 minutes.

## "We Can't Afford It Right Now"

- Mention that it's just a free estimate
- Highlight potential future savings (catching damage early)
- "Wouldn't you like to see how much it would cost to have a professional do it?"
- Pivot to financing (see pricing.md)

## "I Can Do the Painting Myself"

"Wouldn't you like to see how much it costs for a professional to do it?"

Compare cost to DIY in time, materials, and risk of needing a redo.

## "Can I Get a Flyer?"

Provide the flyer AND offer your contact number for follow-ups.

"What we normally do is [give you my number, schedule a follow-up next week, etc.]."

## "We Already Have a Painter"

"Wouldn't you like to see how much it would cost to have a professional do it?"

Frame it as a sanity check on what they're already paying, not as switching providers.

## The "If/Then Would You at Least Consider" Structure

For pre-close objections, this is the universal bridge back to the close:

"So if we [go out there / find a price that fits / agree on the timing], would you at least consider [reserving a spot / getting on my schedule / taking the 10% discount] today?"

This earns a small yes that opens the door to the bigger yes.

## Objection Handling Principles

- Stay calm. Never argumentative.
- Never make the customer look bad or feel unknowledgeable.
- Use test questions: "If... then..." closes.
- Be genuine. People don't like salesmen.
- Address objections BEFORE they come up (preemptive framing during the Client Manual).
- Get as many "yes" responses as possible while presenting. Momentum compounds.
- Always ask for the job again.
- Every "no" gets you one step closer to yes.
