# College Works Sales Methodology

## The In-Home Estimate Process (9 Steps)
The audio recording covers the in-home appointment. These are the 9 steps the rep should execute during that appointment, in order:

1. **Review and prepare the initial sit down** — Get to the kitchen table with both decision makers
2. **Client Manual Presentation** — Build rapport (FORC), trigger questions, walk through Client Manual
3. **Pre-Close** — The $100k Pre-Close before going outside, including ball-parking
4. **Walk-around** — NSSC throughout, develop need, create urgency
5. **Estimating/Bidding** — Pricing back at the kitchen table
6. **Writing up the Estimate** — All bullet points, individual prices, total
7. **Closing the Deal** — Money question, present estimate, closing question, get authorization
8. **Closing Objection Handling** — 5-Step Close if "think about it" comes up
9. **Sales Journal / Report Card** — Self-reflection at end of appointment. The rep submitting the recording for AI coaching fulfills this step automatically.

Note: Post-appointment activities (callbacks, thank you letters, H-Marketing, lawn signs, 1st/15th calls) happen after the in-home estimate ends and aren't evaluable from the audio.

## Critical Foundation: Both Decision Makers
More than 80% of household decisions are made at the kitchen table with both spouses present. Getting both decision makers at the table is non-negotiable.

Opening line: "Hi [name], can myself, you and [spouse] grab a seat at the kitchen table so I can show you some items before we go outside to walk around the house? Thanks!"

## Building Rapport (FORC)
After arriving at the kitchen table, PUT THE CLIENT MANUAL AWAY. The focus is on you and the homeowner. Minimum 15 minutes of rapport building.

Building rapport is like traveling down a river. Choose a stream to go down. If it doesn't look good, back up and try another. Eventually you'll find a great stream.

FORC = the 4 streams:
- **Family**: kids, spouse, where they're from
- **Occupation**: what they do, how long, what they enjoy
- **Recreation**: hobbies, sports, interests, vacation
- **Community**: how long here, neighbors, area

Pattern: 2 questions, 1 share. Listen more than talk. Feed off their answers. Get little "yes" responses.

## Client Manual Presentation
After 15+ minutes of rapport, pull out the Client Manual. Start with trigger questions to build credibility:

### Trigger Questions (in order)
1. "What qualities are you looking for in someone working on your home?"
2. "Have you worked with a contractor before? Was it good or bad?"
3. "What are you looking for in a paint job: quality, service, or price?"
4. "Have you thought about colors?"
5. "Have you heard of College Works?"
6. "How long do you plan to stay in this home?"

### Client Manual Content Flow
- About Me page (your vision, why you're doing this)
- College Works overview (20+ years, references, awards)
- Color selection process (test patches, design consultation)
- Insurance and credentials
- Product quality (Sherwin-Williams Emerald/Duration/SuperPaint)
- Warranty (1-year standard, optional 3-year)
- Booking process (10% off discount, 25% deposit, balance on completion)
- Sample contract walkthrough

## The $100k Pre-Close
Before going outside for the walk-around:

1. Genuine thanks for their time
2. Explain the rest of the process (you're the "human calculator")
3. Show sample contract from similar booked project. Explain 10% off, 25% down
4. State your goal: "to impress you and get you in my schedule"
5. Money question / ball-parking
6. Ask: "Besides price, is there any reason why you wouldn't be able to today?"
7. Pre-close statement: "So both of you, come out with me to do the walk-around"

Handle ANY objections that come up here. Everything except price gets handled before the walk-around.

## Walk-Around (Where NSSC Happens)
Both decision makers must come outside. This is where the sale is made. Use NSSC throughout.

### Need Satisfaction Selling Cycle (NSSC)
6 steps, used for each issue you identify on the walk-around:

**1. IDENTIFY NEED** — Point out the issue
Example: "Have you noticed the peeling on your siding here?"

**2. DEVELOP THE NEED** — Build why it matters (past, present, future, cause and effect, discuss 2 per side)
Example: "If this isn't taken care of, the peeling will continue and moisture will seep underneath the siding. That makes the peeling worse. Eventually the siding needs to be replaced, which could cost more than the paint job itself."

**3. SHOW SOLUTION** — How you'll fix it
Example: "What we'll do is scrape off the flaking paint, sand the surface smooth, then prime the bare areas before painting."

**4. SUMMARIZE BENEFITS** — 3 categories per side: benefits of you as manager, benefits of College Works, benefits of getting it done

**5. ASSUME THE SALE** — "When" not "if"
Example: "When we do this for you..."

**6. CLOSE, CLOSE, CLOSE** — Get confirmation
Example: "Do you want that? Is that what you're looking for?"

## Closing (Back at Kitchen Table)

### Setup
- Both homeowners present
- Put the contract AWAY initially
- Build brief rapport first (lowers their defensive shield)

### The Money Question (Pre-Close Rapport)
"Before we go into that, I just have one question. Knowing that I am looking to be very successful in the future and that you have lived longer than I have, what is one piece of advice you can give me?"

This puts them in a giving mindset before the close.

### Present the Estimate
Go top to bottom. All bullet points. Individual prices and total price.

### The Closing Question
"So the price is X. How would you like to take advantage of the 10% coupon today, via check or credit card?"

If check: golden. Make sure 25% deposit is to College Works Painting.
If credit card: great, then mention the 3% fee. They can re-decide but they've already committed.

## The 5-Step Close (When Client Says "Think About It")
The 5-Step Close uncovers the REAL objection. Ask each question in order:

**Question 1**: "Is this the EXACT job you are looking for? Anything you'd add or subtract?"
- If NO: Issue is walk-around or finding what client really wants

**Question 2**: "After getting to know me today, do you trust that I and my painters can deliver?"
- If NO: Issue is rapport, client manual, or walk-around confidence

**Question 3**: "Do you feel comfortable with College Works as a company, our insurance and guarantees?"
- If NO: Issue is client manual, building company reputation

**Question 4**: "Is this price in your budget this year?"
- If NO: Issue is price structure or rapport. Ask "What did you think it would cost?"

**Question 5**: "Would you agree that the job is getting done this year?"
- If NO: Issue is developing need on walk-around, creating buy-in

If YES to all 5, close with the ultimate logic:
"So if this is the exact job you want, you feel comfortable with me managing it, you trust College Works, it's in your budget, and it's definitely getting done this year, let's go ahead and save you 10% and get you exactly what you're looking for."

Then GET THE AUTHORIZATION AND PICK UP THE CHECK.

## Field Position Theory (10 Steps to a Won Estimate)

Every estimate moves through a defined sequence. Each step earns the right to the next. Skipping steps is the single most common reason estimates fail to close.

1. **I-Call / Setting at the Door**: set up a winnable estimate with both decision-makers present
2. **Confirmation Call**: typically Thursday before the estimate
3. **Pre-Estimate Packet**: drop off materials, second touchpoint, lets them see the rep again
4. **Rapport**: 20+ minutes minimum on first 25 estimates, 30-45 minutes after
5. **Trigger Questions**: transition from rapport into the Client Manual
6. **Client Manual**: credibility, differentiation, paint products, warranties, before/after, reference letters
7. **Pre-Close**: "Besides the price, is there anything that would keep you from reserving one of my last few spots and taking advantage of the 10% discount today?"
8. **Walk-Around**: 4 sides of the house, NSSC applied per side
9. **Ballpark**: give ranges per option before finalizing numbers
10. **Present Contract + Close**: walk through options, emphasize discount, close

## Needs Satisfaction Selling Cycle (NSSC)

NSSC is the spine of every sales conversation. The six steps cycle through every side of the walk-around and through the closing conversation.

1. Identify the need
2. Develop the need
3. Show your solution
4. Summarize benefits
5. Assume the sale
6. Close, close, close

Throughout every step: build rapport, be assumptive, handle objections.

## 200k Triangle

The 200k Triangle is the visual reminder for NSSC. The interns who hit $200k consistently apply all six steps every time, not just steps 1, 3 and 5.

## F.O.R.D. (Rapport Framework)

The rapport framework for the sit-down and walk-around. Listen for what they want to talk about and stay there.

- **F**amily (kids, dogs, extended family)
- **O**ccupation
- **R**ecreation
- **D**reams

### Rivers and Streams Principle

Treat conversation topics as rivers and streams. When you find one they care about, follow it. A small stream of interest can turn into a waterfall of rapport. The best way to book a job is to get them to like you. Look around the house and at their Facebook beforehand to find topics.

First question to ask: "How long have you lived here?"

## The 6-Step Estimate Process

The estimate runs in this order. Skipping the sit-down or going straight to the walk-around is the single biggest cause of low close rates.

1. **Build rapport**: F.O.R.D., conversation starters, mirror their energy
2. **Stump speech**: your why, your goal for the visit, set expectations
3. **Client Manual**: trigger questions, listen, dig deeper, address needs
4. **Discuss colors**: visualization questions, design consultation, two-coat framing
5. **Build urgency**: scheduling, sample agreement, 10% pre-close
6. **Explain pricing**: labor, materials, insurance, hours (clarify the homeowner controls the hours)
7. **Discuss problem areas**: walk-around, NSSC per side, two paint-related issues per side

## Walk-Around (4-Side Structure)

Each side of the house gets a focus. NSSC is applied on every side.

- **Side 1: Wash**: power wash (or hose plus wet-rag for lead). Compare caulking vs no caulking.
- **Side 2: Prep process**: compare power wash to scrape vs hand scraping. Compare sanding vs primer only (no sanding on pre-1978). Compare priming vs just painting. Compare paint types.
- **Side 3: Colors and prep**: pick another piece of prep to explain
- **Side 4: Pre-Close**: "If I include everything we talked about and it's a reasonable price, would you like to reserve a spot today and take advantage of that 10% discount?"

Two paint-related issues identified per side. Build rapport at the corners of the property.

## Five Types of Closes

Top performers cycle through these based on the homeowner's energy. None of them are tricks; they're invitations to a yes.

- **Easy lead-in:** "So all I need is a deposit and your autograph here, and we can reserve your spot for the summer."
- **Blank order:** Set the pen down on the contract and wait.
- **Major/minor close:** "Will you be looking to include the shed in this as well?"
- **Alternate choice:** "Would you like us to schedule you for early summer, mid-summer, or late summer?"
- **Whatever it takes:** "What's it going to take for me to paint your home this summer?"

## The 5-Step Close

When a homeowner says "think about it" (which is not a real objection), walk them through this sequence. Each yes builds momentum to the final yes.

1. Do you like the company and what it has to offer?
2. Do you think I am the right person for this job?
3. Is this the type of quality you're looking for?
4. Does this price sound fair for what I am offering?
5. Do you want this job done this year?

If they say yes to the first four, the fifth becomes hard to say no to.

## Key Coaching Principles

- Under promise, over deliver
- Manage expectations
- Speak professionally and confidently
- Assume the sale
- Mirror your client's energy
- Be the FIRST estimate (aggressive marketing wins this) AND close on the spot (callbacks are much harder)
- The most crucial time to close is BEFORE the estimate is presented (pre-close at the end of the sit-down)
- Use the "If/Then would you at least consider" structure to bridge any objection back to the close
