Core Principle
Score against realistic field excellence, not theoretical perfection.
A top-performing College Works rep, executing a real 90-minute estimate with a real homeowner, will not hit theoretical perfection. They will adapt to the homeowner in front of them. They will skip framework steps that don't apply. They will trade some script fidelity for genuine connection. That estimate should score 8-9, not 6-7.
The mistake to avoid: anchoring 10 to "executed every framework flawlessly" and 5 to "average." When that happens, the scale collapses. Top reps score in the high 6s and feel demotivated. Average reps score in the low 5s and don't know how far they actually are from elite execution. The scoring becomes statistically logical and motivationally useless.
The fix: reserve 10 for true mastery moments (rare, earned, single-phase). Reserve 9 for excellent execution across the entire estimate. Treat 8 as the realistic ceiling for most full estimates by top reps. Treat 7 as solid with clear growth paths. Treat 6 and below as real coaching priorities, not "slightly below ideal."
The Score Bands
10: Mastery Moment
Reserved. Award for single moments of true excellence within an estimate, not for full estimates. Examples: a flawless objection handle that converted a hard "no" into "let's do it," a rapport breakthrough where the homeowner shared something deeply personal because the rep earned it, a close that read the room perfectly and landed without friction. A 10 is rare enough that when a rep sees one, they should be able to point to the exact moment and tell their teammates about it.
Most full estimates, even excellent ones, do not contain a 10. That's the point.
9: Excellent Execution Across the Board
This is what an elite rep at the top of their craft looks like across a full phase or category. Framework executed with skill and intention. Adapts to the homeowner in real time. Uses top-performer language naturally (not robotically). Earns the next step. Genuinely hard to fault.
Rare but not unicorn-rare. A top rep should score a 9 in one or two categories per great estimate, not all of them.
8: Strong Top-Rep Performance
The realistic ceiling for most categories in a great estimate. Methodology is clearly being executed. Top-performer behaviors are present. Small refinements are possible (a deeper trigger question, a slightly cleaner pivot, a better-timed pre-close) but nothing major is missing. This is what reps should target.
If a rep scores 8s across the board, they had a great estimate. The overall score should reflect that.
7: Solid Execution With Clear Coaching Opportunities
The rep is running the framework but execution is mechanical or partial. Rapport is functional but not deep. Trigger questions get asked but the rep doesn't dig into the answers. The walk-around hits the four sides but NSSC isn't fully applied per side. The close happens but the pre-close was skipped or rushed. This rep is competent and growing. Coaching focuses on depth, not basics.
This is where most "good" reps land. It is not a bad score. It means "you're doing the work and there's a clear path to top-rep level."
6: Competent But With Real Gaps
The framework is in evidence but key elements are missing or weak. The rep may have built rapport but skipped trigger questions entirely. The client manual may have been a flip-through rather than a conversation. The walk-around may have been a measurement run, not a sales tool. The rep is functioning, but the gaps are likely costing close rate or revenue.
A 6 is the threshold where coaching becomes urgent. The rep needs to address the gaps before the next estimate, not gradually over the summer.
5: Below Standard
Multiple major frameworks skipped or fumbled. Rapport feels transactional. Trigger questions missed. Pre-close not run. Walk-around lacks NSSC structure. The rep is in the door and moving through the motions, but the conversation isn't being engineered toward a sale. Outcome may be acceptable for easy buyers; close rate will lag badly on harder ones.
4: Significant Problems
Methodology execution is the exception, not the rule. The rep is taking estimates without running the system. Often the result of a new rep before training fully lands, or a struggling rep who has stopped trusting the framework.
1-3: Severe Failure
Methodology effectively absent. Behaviors that actively damage the homeowner relationship (interrupting, arguing, defensiveness, dismissiveness). Should be rare. When it appears, the issue is likely deeper than coaching: confidence collapse, lack of preparation, or the rep needs to talk with their DM about whether to continue.
How the Overall Score Should Be Calculated
The overall score is not the arithmetic mean of the category scores. It is a qualitative summary that reflects:

The categories where the rep was strongest (highest score)
The categories where the rep was weakest (lowest score)
Whether the gaps likely affected the outcome
The arc of the estimate (did the rep build momentum or lose it)

A rep who scores 9 / 9 / 8 / 5 / 4 (rapport, client manual, walk-around, pre-close, close) should not get a 7 overall. The first three are elite. The last two are the entire reason the estimate didn't close. The overall score should land around 7 because the strengths are real, but the close failure is the headline. Coaching should focus on the 5 and the 4, not the 9s.
A rep who scores 7 / 7 / 7 / 7 / 7 is much more concerning than the headline number suggests. Consistent mid-range execution across every category often means the rep is going through the motions everywhere. The coaching priority is depth, not any one weakness.
When in doubt, weight the closing-related categories (pre-close, presentation, close, objection handling) more heavily in the overall score. These are where revenue is won or lost.
Outcome Weighting (Sold / Not Sold / Callback)
Outcome matters but does not dominate. A rep can lose a homeowner who was never going to buy from anyone. A rep can sell a homeowner who would have bought from a flyer. The score should reflect execution against the methodology more than outcome.
Guideline: outcome should not shift the overall score by more than ~1 point in either direction.

Sold: the rep gets the benefit of the doubt on close execution if the outcome was clean
Callback: scored on execution, with attention to whether the rep set up the callback well or just deferred a decision they should have closed
Not sold: scored on execution. If execution was elite and the homeowner still didn't buy, the score reflects the elite execution. If execution was the reason, the score reflects that

The coaching narrative explains the score. The score does not explain the coaching.
Common Calibration Mistakes to Avoid
Penalizing for steps that didn't apply. If the homeowner cut the rapport short because they were running to a meeting, the rep adapting to that is not a failure of rapport. It is good reading of the room.
Equating "didn't say the exact script" with "failed the framework." Top reps execute principles, not scripts. A rep who built rapport with their own language scored well on rapport. A rep who recited the script woodenly scored worse.
Treating skipped steps as automatic point loss without context. Sometimes skipping is the right call. The grading question is whether the principle was honored, not whether the step was visible.
Scoring "neutral" moments as low. If the rep transitioned cleanly between phases without anything notable happening, that is not a 5. That is a 7 or 8 if the framework was executed.
Stacking minor gaps into a major deduction. Three small refinements available in a phase is still an 8, not a 6. Real deduction happens when something material was missed or fumbled.
Reference Sentences for Score Communication
When the score is shared with the rep, the language matters as much as the number. Examples:

8.5 overall: "This is strong top-rep work. The growth areas below are the difference between a great estimate and an elite one."
7.2 overall: "You're running the framework. The next level is depth in a few specific places, called out below."
6.0 overall: "The bones are there, but there are real gaps that are likely costing you revenue. Coaching priority: [specific phase]."
4.5 overall: "This estimate needs a reset on fundamentals. Let's get on a call with your DM this week."

The score is the headline. The coaching is the story. Both should match.
