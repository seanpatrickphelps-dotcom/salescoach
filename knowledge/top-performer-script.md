# Top Performer Scripts

> **NEW knowledge base file for collegeworkscoach.com.** Create as `knowledge/top-performer-script.md`. Contains verbatim language patterns from top-performing interns. Use as reference for what high-converting language sounds like in the field. When a rep's transcript shows weak or generic phrasing in any of these moments, this file is what "good" looks like.

## Direct Marketing Pitch (Door-Knock)

"Hey, how are you doing?"

[response]

"Good. My name is [Name], I'm a local college student at [School]. I'm also running a local painting business. I'm going to be painting a lot of houses in the neighborhood and I stopped by to see if you'd like a free estimate."

### Get Them in the Schedule (when answer is "yes")

- "What is your name?"
- "What is your number?"
- "Is there another decision maker? What is their info?"
- "I'll be out here next weekend, what is your schedule on Saturday? Does 8am work?"

## I-Call (Setting the Estimate at the Door)

Primary goals on the I-Call: two decision makers locked in, 25% deposit and 10% discount positioned, buying mindset, WINNABLE estimate.

- Both decision-makers must be present for the full 90-minute estimate
- Position the 25% deposit and 10% pre-season discount up front
- Get them in the buying mindset before they hang up
- The call is to set up a WINNABLE estimate, not to sell

## Confirmation Call (Usually Thursday Before Estimate)

- "Just a couple of reminders. We'll start with a sit-down with both of you for 15 minutes or so, then do a walk around where you can show me what you want estimated."
- "Once I measure it up, I'll come back in and show you a few options and we can see which one makes the most sense for you guys."
- "Pre-season 10% discount if you decide the price is right and you want to reserve a spot in my summer schedule."
- "If you decide to reserve a spot, I only take 25% deposit with the other 75% not due until everything is completed."
- "Double check that time works for the both of you to sit down at the beginning and end. I'm a busy college student and driving from [city], so I really do appreciate your willingness to both make that time work."
- "I want to make sure you are BOTH going to be there. I understand life is busy and it is for me too. I'll be driving 2.5+ hours so as long as you respect my time I do appreciate it. See you soon."

## Trigger Questions (Transition from Rapport into the Client Manual)

These are the questions that move the conversation from "getting to know you" into the value conversation. Listen carefully to the answers and tailor the rest of the estimate to what they tell you.

### "How has your experience been working with contractors?"

Find a bad experience and elaborate on why they won't have that with you.

- If they have worked with contractors before: "What was your experience like?"
  - Good: "What can I also do to give you a good experience?"
  - Bad: "I'm here to give you a good experience" (hit it hard, make it personal)
- If they have not worked with contractors: jump to the average contractor rating
  - "Average contractor customer satisfaction rating is between 40-45%. We're over 90%."

### "What matters most to you in a paint job? Best price? Best quality? Best service? Or somewhere in the middle?"

This question lets the homeowner sort themselves into a tier.

- "With higher price comes higher quality. You can't pay for a Honda Civic and get a Ferrari's quality, but you will never pay for the Ferrari and get the Honda Civic."
- "I don't have a bad option because I use the best material on the market. I have a good, better, and best option."

Quality and Price are directly related. Quality = length of time the job lasts.

If they say PRICE: "I can give you a bunch of options to see what fits your budget. Sacrifice a little quality with a cheaper price, and vice versa."

If they say QUALITY: "That's why I'm here. I'm doing this for reference letters. I'm not the cheapest price because I can't give you the best quality at the lowest price. We are partners with Sherwin Williams, so best quality."

If they say SERVICE: "Nobody is going to beat me in this category." Then specifics: nightly walkaround, avid communication, just down the road.

### "How long do you plan on staying here?"

Drives the conversation toward longer warranties and better paint.

### "Have you thought about colors?"

You want them excited about colors. Use this to introduce the two-coat conversation:

- Color change ALWAYS = 2 coats
- Color staying the same: "Have you thought about number of coats?"
- "2 coats = double life expectancy for half the price"

### "Have you heard of College Works Painting before?"

- Yes: "Okay, how have you heard about it?"
- No: "Great, let me tell you about it. The nice thing is you're working with CWP but you're working directly with me, who has the backing of CWP. And to tell you about me..."

Transition into the stump speech.

## Stump Speech Framing

Brag about yourself and explain your "why." Differentiate from peers without putting them down explicitly.

Example structure:

"A little bit more about me: I'm from [city]. I grew up playing sports and decided to go to [school]. I wanted to do something different and set myself up for a career. While my roommates are playing video games, drinking, and hungover, I'm out here trying to earn your business..."

End with the goal:

"My goal for today is that you're impressed by me, we find an option in your budget, and you reserve one of my limited spots for the summer and take advantage of my 10% discount."

## Awards / Internship Credibility Framing

Briefly skip past awards but anchor credibility:

- "Here's some of the awards we've got from quality, the internship, etc."
- Mention the interview process to get the position: "Thousands of people apply to this. The best of the best college students."

## Who Does the Painting (Hiring Story)

- "It's not me."
- "I hire from the same pool as other companies, but I don't hire based on painting experience."
- "I hire based on the skills you can't teach: accountable, responsible, respectful, coachable."
- "I do the grandma test. I won't hire them if I wouldn't introduce them to my grandma."
- "We teach good people how to paint. Painting isn't rocket science and is very teachable."
- "My painters will not be practicing on your house. They will be professionally trained by Sherwin Williams."

## Safety Framing

When asked about or introducing safety:

- "Safety is our number one concern."
- "Every time we get up on a roof, we will always be wearing a harness."
- "This is important so they don't fall off, but it's important for the quality of your job as well."
- "Always wear hard hats."

Important facts to mention:

- "We work with thousands of clients per year"
- "$1,000,000+ in liability insurance"
- "Full workers compensation"
- "A+ rating with the Better Business Bureau"

## Pre-Close (Before the Walk-Around)

After the Client Manual, before going outside:

"So besides the price, is there anything that would keep you from reserving one of my last few spots for the summer and taking advantage of the 10% discount I am offering TODAY?"

### If They Have an Objection at Pre-Close

Use an "If/then would you at least consider" structure:

"So if we do go out there and agree that it needs to get done this year, would you at least consider getting in my schedule today?"

## Ballpark (After Walk-Around, Before Going Back Inside)

At the door, to both homeowners:

- Ask a random anchoring question first: "Is it okay if we keep ladders here overnight in the back WHEN we do your project?" (the assumption is built into the question)
- Give ranges for each option ($500 below and $1,000 over the real number)
- "Are these prices what you were expecting?"
- "I'm going to finalize the exact numbers. It'll take about 10 minutes and I will come back inside and show you all of the specifics for the different options."

## Closing (Walk Through Contract)

- "Is your information correct?"
- "Here is my info. Call me, not the office."
- Walk through areas estimated and what you will do
- Walk through bullet point notes (nightly walk around, daily clean up, etc.)
- Walk them through options. Emphasize discount.

## Service Differentiation Language

When emphasizing what makes CWP different:

- "Local Service, with big-business security."
- "I'm only working with 20-25 clients this summer. I don't work with a ton of people, so I'm not mass producing projects and cutting corners."
- "I have a personal Sherwin Williams representative specific to me."
- "I'm going to do a daily/nightly walk around with you to ensure we're on the same page. Do you think you'll be satisfied with the project if you're satisfied at the end of every day?"

## Quality and Prep Language

- "60% of a job's durability is directly correlated to the prep work. We do NOT skip this."
- "80-90% of other contractors power wash to remove peeling paint. It's fast and cheap, but it blasts moisture into the wood and causes the paint job to fail. Which defeats the entire purpose of a paint job: keeping moisture out."
- "Hand scraping is more time-consuming, but it's the best way and enhances longevity. Anything that flicks up with a fingernail comes off."
- "Many contractors skip this step. Many clients opt out just because it's more expensive. The 'paint and primer in one' paints make people think they don't need to prime. That's not the case. Paint can't adhere to bare wood."
- "Is that the type of quality you're looking for?"

## Reference Letter Framing

"My entire goal of working with you is to impress you enough with my service that you will want to write me a reference letter. I'm going to use these when I apply for my job in the future and compete with other people in the job search."

## Warranty Mention (Keep Brief)

"We do ensure our work with warranties. Any 2-coat gets a 3-year warranty. A one-coat gets a one-year warranty."

## Consumer Reports Mention (Light Touch)

Don't stress this page too much. If the homeowner asks about pricing context: "The average cost to paint a house is $4,000 to $10,500, depending on area, paint, and prep."
