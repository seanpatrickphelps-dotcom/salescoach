# Pricing & Financing (Sales Conversation)

> **For collegeworkscoach.com knowledge base.** Save as `knowledge/pricing.md` (new file). The CWP app handles the estimating math, so this file covers only what reps SAY to homeowners about pricing during sales conversations: service plans, 2-coat positioning, financing, and what to turn down at the door.

## Standard vs. Premium Service Plans

| | Standard | Premium |
|---|---|---|
| Coats | 1 | 2 (full houses only) |
| Lifespan | 5-8 yrs (good paint), 8-9 yrs (premium paint) | 10-12 yrs (top-grade paint) |
| Warranty | 1 year | 3 years |

The Premium 3-year warranty is among the longest in the industry. Reps should be able to walk a homeowner through these two tiers without notes.

## 2-Coat Positioning

The rep doesn't need to do the math; they need to position the value:

- **Color change ALWAYS = 2 coats** (mandatory, not optional)
- **Same color repaint:** "2 coats = double life expectancy for half the price"
- The second coat is much cheaper than the first because prep is already done and painters are already on site
- Position 2-coats as the responsible long-term choice, not the upsell

## Ballpark Approach (Before Final Numbers)

After the walk-around, before going back inside to present, the rep gives ranges (not exact numbers) to take the temperature:

- Give ranges per option, not exact numbers
- "Are these prices what you were expecting?"
- "I'm going to finalize the exact numbers. About 10 minutes."

The ballpark prevents a price-shocked reaction at the contract.

## What CWP Does Not Bid

If a rep is at the door or in an I-Call and the homeowner describes one of these, decline politely and don't book the estimate:

- Government projects (schools, etc.)
- Projects requiring scaffolding
- Buildings taller than three stories
- Anything requiring complete paint removal
- Projects with overly challenging ladder placements
- Subcontracted work

## Financing (Payment Plans)

Position as "payment plans," not "financing." More than 70% of home improvement projects over $2,500 use some form of financing.

### Why Financing Closes More Jobs

- 30% increase in average job size
- 18% increase in booking rate
- Lower deposit barrier (10% instead of 25%)

### Payment Plan Structure

| Minimum Amount | Term | Interest Rate |
|---|---|---|
| $2,500 | 5 years | 11.9% |
| $3,500 | 10 years | 11.9% |
| $7,500 | 12 years | 11.9% |

Monthly payment rule of thumb: $15-$20 per month per $1,000 financed.

$5,000 financed = roughly $60-$106/month.

### Financing Talking Points (Verbatim)

- "A lot of people do this to be able to afford a lot of things nowadays."
- "It's a lot of money when you look at it as a whole. Easier to think about it like Netflix, your car payment, or your phone bill."
- "Many people finance anything over $10k."
- "11.99% interest. Higher than a mortgage, but lower than a credit card."
- "90 days deferred payment, but interest does still accumulate during that window."
- "No prepayment penalty. You can pay it off whenever."
- "Soft credit pull until the project is completed."
- "If you finance, I only need to take 10% deposit instead of 25%, and you finance the remainder."

### When to Pivot to Financing

When the homeowner says any of these, financing is the right next move:

- "We can't afford it right now"
- "That's out of my budget"
- "The price is higher than we thought"
- Any hesitation tied to dollar amount rather than need

## Job Size Minimums (Relevant to Financing Pitch)

- General estimate minimum: $1,000 job size
- Financing minimum: $3,500 (or $2,500 after 10% discount)
- Long-term financing (12-year): $7,500 minimum
