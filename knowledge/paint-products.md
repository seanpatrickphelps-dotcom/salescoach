## Product Tiers (Good / Better / Best)

### A-100 (Don't Sell)

- Price reference: $25/gal
- Position as the budget option, but steer away
- "Doesn't hold up. No warranty."
- Used for pricing reference only, not actually recommended

### Super Paint

- Price: ~$35/gal
- Thickness: 0.7mm dry
- Lifespan: 5-6 years with 1 coat, 9-12 years with 2 coats
- Position: "Sherwin sold this for a very long time. Not the best, not the worst."
- Demo line: "Dip your finger in it and pull it up. It drips like water."
- The honest middle option

### Resilience (Avoid)

- Same quality as Super Paint, more expensive
- Designed for warmer climates
- No added value for most jobs
- Don't recommend

### Duration

- Price: $55-$65/gal
- Thickness: 1.3mm dry
- Lifespan: 7-8 years with 1 coat, 12-14 years with 2 coats
- Position: "Same chemical properties as Super Paint, but they take the moisture out so it's thicker. Considered the best paint in the industry for a long time. Lasts a couple years longer."

### Emerald / Rain Refresh

- Price: $70-$80/gal
- Thickness: 1.7mm dry
- Lifespan: 10-12 years with 1 coat, 15-16 years with 2 coats
- Position: "Best paint on the market. Covers very well. Very good at preventing fading with dark colors and bright colors."
- Key feature (self-cleaning): "Every time it rains, it washes itself. Helps with dirt and mildew."

## Why Higher Paint Solids Matter

- Higher resin-to-solvent ratio = thicker paint
- Thicker paint = more uniform color coverage
- Thicker paint = better protection against weather
- Higher titanium dioxide content = better color coverage and durability
- Ideal for high-traffic areas and exterior surfaces

## 2-Coat Framing

When the homeowner is on the fence about 2 coats:

- Color change ALWAYS = 2 coats (mandatory, not optional)
- For same-color repaints: "2 coats = double life expectancy for half the price"
- Position 2-coats as the responsible long-term choice, not the upsell

### 2-Coat Pricing Formula

- First coat = full painting price (after prep is subtracted from total)
- Second coat = 60% of first coat painting price
- (Total job price minus prep cost) = 1st coat painting price
- 1st coat painting price multiplied by 60% = 2nd coat painting price

## Stain vs Paint

- Paint seals the wood completely
- Stain shows the wood grain through the color
- Solid-body stain: opaque, covers old paint, more affordable
- Semi-transparent stain: shows wood grain, more expensive, multi-step application

## Common Paint Problems (Diagnostic Vocabulary)

When pointing out failures during the walk-around, name them precisely:

- **Peeling**: paint separating from surface
- **Flaking**: small pieces lifting off
- **Bubbling**: trapped moisture causing blisters
- **Alligatoring**: cracked pattern resembling alligator skin (paint problem you can't solve, needs full removal)
- **Chalking**: powdery residue on surface
- **Intercoat peeling**: top coat separating from bottom coat
- **Stucco patch failure**: visible repair lines
- **Wood rot**: structural failure (not a paint problem, carpentry issue)

Naming the failure precisely builds credibility. "Your fascia is alligatoring" is sharper than "your paint looks bad."
